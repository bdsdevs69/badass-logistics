Dedicated Freight Lanes: When a Project Needs Its Own Trucks

Booking forty loads one at a time is how a relocation falls behind. Here is how dedicated lanes work and when a project needs one.

FIELD GUIDE BY THE CREW ✓

Project Freight · By the Badass Logistics crew · September 17, 2026

// quick answer

A dedicated freight lane reserves truck capacity on a specific route for a set period, instead of buying each load on the spot market. It makes sense when a project has predictable, repeated moves between the same places — plant relocations, phased equipment deliveries, warehouse-to-site runs — and when a late or missing truck costs more than the capacity. Planning a lane means sizing the volume and cadence, matching equipment to the freight, and syncing delivery windows with the crews loading and unloading.

One load is easy to book. Forty loads between the same two buildings over three weeks is a different problem. Book them one at a time and you get a different truck every day, a different trailer type than the last one, drivers who don't know either site, and arrival times nobody can plan a crew around. That's the problem dedicated lanes solve.

What is a dedicated freight lane?

A dedicated lane is truck capacity committed to a specific route for a defined period. Instead of posting each shipment and taking whatever truck is available at whatever rate the market sets that day, the capacity is arranged up front — a set number of trucks, on a set schedule, running the same origin and destination.

For industrial projects, "dedicated" usually means dedicated to the project: trucks cycling between a plant being emptied and a plant being filled, or between a staging warehouse and a job site, for as long as the project needs them.

Dedicated vs spot vs contract freight — what's the difference?

Three ways to buy truck capacity

  • Spot freight — each load bought individually at current market conditions. Flexible, but availability and timing vary load to load.
  • Contract freight — expected volume on a lane agreed in advance, often over months. More predictable, but capacity isn't reserved truck by truck.
  • Dedicated capacity — trucks and drivers committed to your lane or project for a period. The most predictable, and the most planning up front.

None is always better. A one-off shipment belongs on the spot market. A steady, predictable project with a schedule that crews depend on is where dedicated capacity earns its keep.

When does a project need dedicated capacity?

  • Plant relocations and consolidations. Many loads between two fixed sites, sequenced to teardown and reinstall. See plant relocation.
  • Phased equipment deliveries. A production line, data center, or hospital build-out taking equipment in a planned order as spaces become ready.
  • Warehouse-to-site runs. Staged or transloaded freight moving to a job site daily or weekly. See what transloading is.
  • Tight install schedules. When rigging crews, cranes, and other trades are scheduled around deliveries, a missing truck idles far more than the truck itself.

A useful test: add up what a late load actually costs the project — crews waiting, cranes on standby, a line down longer. If that's more than the premium for reliable capacity, the lane should be dedicated.

How do you plan a dedicated lane?

1. Size the volume and cadence

Start from the asset list and schedule. How many loads, over how many days, and how many per day can the loading crew actually load and the receiving crew actually set? A lane that delivers faster than the site can absorb just moves the backlog to the dock.

2. Match equipment to the freight

Precision machines ride air-ride. Tall or crane-loaded pieces go on flatbeds or step decks. Crated parts and sensitive electronics often go enclosed. A lane may need a mix of equipment types, planned load by load. For the tradeoffs, see LTL vs FTL freight and shipping machinery on a flatbed.

3. Set loading and delivery windows

Both ends need windows the crews can hit, including dock limits, site access rules, and quiet hours. Consistent windows are one of the biggest advantages of a dedicated lane.

4. Build weight and dimensions into every load

Each load should be planned to run legal and arrive ready to unload in install order, without double-handling on site.

5. Plan for change

Real projects slip. Decide in advance how cadence gets adjusted, how much notice is needed, and who makes the call — so a schedule change becomes a phone call instead of a scramble.

What goes wrong when a big project runs on spot trucks?

  • Trucks arrive before the crew is ready — or after they've gone home.
  • The wrong trailer shows up for the next piece in the sequence.
  • Machines arrive out of order and get handled twice.
  • Drivers unfamiliar with the sites lose time at gates and docks.
  • Capacity disappears during tight market weeks, right when the project needs it.

Bottom line

  • A dedicated lane reserves truck capacity on a route for the length of a project.
  • It fits predictable, repeated moves where a missed truck costs more than the capacity.
  • Plan volume, equipment, windows, and change rules before the first truck runs.

Frequently asked questions

What is a dedicated lane in trucking?
Truck capacity committed to a specific route for a set period, rather than booking each load individually on the spot market.
Is dedicated freight more expensive than spot freight?
It can cost more per load in some markets and less in others. The real comparison is total project cost, including crews and equipment waiting when a spot truck is late or unavailable.
How many loads justify a dedicated lane?
There is no fixed number. It depends on how predictable the freight is, how tight the schedule is, and what a missed truck costs the project.
Can a dedicated lane use different trailer types?
Yes. Project lanes often mix air-ride, flatbed, step deck, and enclosed equipment depending on what each load needs.
What is the difference between contract and dedicated freight?
Contract freight agrees expected volume on a lane in advance. Dedicated capacity commits specific trucks and drivers to the lane or project for a period.
