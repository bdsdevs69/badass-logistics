Chiller, Boiler & HVAC Rigging

Mechanical equipment always seems to live in the worst possible place: on a roof, in a basement behind a door half its size, or in a penthouse the building grew around. We rig chillers, boilers, cooling towers, rooftop units, and air handlers in and out — by crane, by skates, or in pieces when that's the only way through.

Quick answer: HVAC and chiller rigging is the removal, relocation, and installation of heavy mechanical equipment — chillers, boilers, cooling towers, rooftop units, air handlers, and heat exchangers. Badass Logistics plans crane lifts onto roofs and into mechanical yards, skates equipment into basements and mechanical rooms, and coordinates teardown and reassembly with the mechanical contractor when a unit won't fit the opening.

The hard part is getting to the mechanical room

Most chiller and boiler replacements aren't hard because of weight. They're hard because of access. The original unit went in before the walls, the roof deck, or the building next door existed. Now the replacement has to go through whatever openings are left — or over the top of the building on a crane.

Every HVAC rigging job starts with that route. We look at the old unit's way out and the new unit's way in: roof structure and crane reach, street and sidewalk access, door and corridor sizes, freight elevator capacity, stairs, and floor loading in mechanical rooms. Then we pick the method — a crane lift, skates and jacks, or both — and plan the old-out, new-in sequence so the building is without heating or cooling for as short a time as possible.

When a unit simply won't fit, the answer is usually to take it apart. Many chillers and air handlers can be split into sections, moved in pieces, and reassembled in place. We coordinate that with your mechanical contractor or the manufacturer, who handle refrigerant recovery, piping, and controls. Our guide to moving a boiler or chiller covers the basics.

three routes

Up and over, through, or in pieces

Almost every mechanical equipment job comes down to one of three approaches — sometimes all three on the same building:

Crane over the top

Rooftop units, chillers, and cooling towers picked from the street or a lot onto the roof. That means a lift plan, a checked roof structure at the set location, and often an early-morning street or sidewalk closure.

Through the building

Boilers and chillers moved on skates and jacks through loading areas, corridors, and freight elevators, with floor protection and load checks along the path.

Knock-down and reassemble

Equipment split at the factory joints or flanges, moved in sections through the openings available, and reassembled in the mechanical room with the manufacturer or contractor.

replacements

Planning an old-out, new-in replacement

Replacements add a second problem to the move: the building still needs heat or cooling. Good planning keeps the downtime window short.

  • Old unit drained and disconnected, with refrigerant recovery by the mechanical contractor
  • Old unit rigged out and removed before or on the same day the new one arrives
  • New unit delivered to match the rigging window instead of sitting on site
  • Crane and closures scheduled for low-traffic hours
  • New unit set on its curb, rails, or pad and positioned for piping and electrical

For big central-plant replacements with multiple units, we can stage deliveries as project freight so each unit arrives when its space is cleared.

questions

Chiller & HVAC rigging FAQ

How do you get a chiller onto a roof?
Usually with a crane lift from the street or a lot, after checking crane reach, ground conditions, and the roof structure at the set location. Closures and timing are planned into the lift.
What if a new chiller won't fit through the door?
Many chillers and air handlers can be split into sections, moved in pieces, and reassembled in place with the manufacturer or mechanical contractor. We plan that route during the site walk.
Do you handle refrigerant recovery and piping?
No. Refrigerant, piping, electrical, and controls are handled by your mechanical contractor or the manufacturer. We rig the equipment out and in and coordinate the timing with them.
Can you remove an old boiler from a basement?
Yes — on skates and jacks through the building, in sections if needed, or through an opening created by your contractor.
How long does a rooftop unit replacement take?
The lift itself is often short. The time goes into planning, closures, disconnects, and connections, which we schedule to keep the building's downtime short.
