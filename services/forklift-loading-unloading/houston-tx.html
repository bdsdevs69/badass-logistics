Machinery loading and unloading in Houston, TX

Most loading damage happens in a few seconds: forks set under the wrong point, a top-heavy machine tipped on a slope, a forklift picking a load past its rated load center. In a metro built on energy & petrochemical, Ship Channel refineries, machines and crated equipment arrive and leave every day at buildings that were never laid out for them — no dock, a gravel lot, a container that has to be emptied from the doors inward.

Badass Logistics treats loading and unloading as rigging, not labor. We verify weight and center of gravity, size the forklift to the actual load and ground, and position and secure the load for the carrier — and when the machine has to go further than the tailgate, the crew skates it through the door and sets it in place. We don't rent forklifts; we provide the crew, the lift, and the gear as one job. Forklift vs crane for machine loading →

A lot of what gets unloaded in Houston arrives in containers. The Port of Houston on the Houston Ship Channel anchors the largest petrochemical complex in the Western Hemisphere, handling breakbulk, bulk and project cargo. Served by Union Pacific, BNSF and CPKC, all interchanging through the Port Terminal Railroad Association switching network along the Ship Channel. The receiving end is usually the Houston Ship Channel industrial corridor through Pasadena, Deer Park and Galena Park, the Baytown petrochemical complex, and the Jacintoport Industrial District.

Equipment we rig for Houston's energy and petrochemical plants

Houston's industrial base — energy & petrochemical, Ship Channel refineries — runs on equipment that doesn't move like freight. Turnaround windows, site safety rules, and heavy concentrated loads on process pads set the schedule. Typical load and unload jobs for Houston customers include:

Compressors and pump skidsHeat exchangers and vesselsTransformers and switchgearGenerators and turbinesProcess skids and modulesControl buildings and e-houses

The dock is the variable. Houston buildings are heavy industrial process plants and tank farms along the Ship Channel built around fixed piping runs and pipe racks that constrain crane and rigging access — so one delivery backs into a dock leveler and the next comes off a trailer at ground level onto a lot. What comes off those trucks is mostly pressure vessels and reactors, distillation columns, industrial valves and pumps, and pipe fabrication equipment, and every one of them gets a weight and center-of-gravity check before the forks go in.

How we price a Houston loading job

Every machinery loading & unloading job in Houston, TX is quoted from the actual work, not a rate sheet. These are the things that move the number — send them with your request and you'll get a real quote back fast:

  • Weight, dimensions, and center of gravity of each piece
  • Trailer or container type, and whether there is a dock
  • Ground conditions and the path from the truck to the door
  • Loading, blocking, and securement requirements for the carrier
  • Whether the crew also moves it inside, sets it, or stages it

Photos of the equipment, its nameplate, and the path out of the building save a site visit on smaller Houston jobs. For bigger moves we walk both sites before we price anything.

Houston Loading FAQ

Do you offer machinery loading and unloading in Houston?
Yes — machine load-outs, equipment receiving, container unloads, and dock-less deliveries throughout Houston, TX and the surrounding metro, serving its energy and petrochemical base. Get a quote →
Do you rent forklifts in Houston?
No. Loading and unloading is provided as rigging work — the crew, a forklift sized to the load, and the rigging gear together — not equipment rental.
How much do loading crews cost in Houston?
There is no flat rate for machinery loading & unloading — the price comes from the equipment, the access at both ends, the crew and gear, distance, and setting work. Send the equipment list and both sites and we'll quote your Houston job fast, usually the same day.
Can you unload heavy equipment without a loading dock?
Yes. We unload at ground level from open-deck and enclosed trailers with forklifts, rigging gear, or a crane, depending on the load and the site.
Can you move the equipment into the building too?
Yes. The crew can take it through the door on skates, set it, and level it — or stage it until the floor is ready.
What do you load and unload for Houston plants?
Mostly pressure vessels and reactors, distillation columns, industrial valves and pumps, and pipe fabrication equipment for the metro's petrochemical processing, oil and gas equipment fabrication, plastics and resin manufacturing, and industrial valve and pipe fabrication — machines, crates, and skids on and off trucks and containers, and into the building when needed.
