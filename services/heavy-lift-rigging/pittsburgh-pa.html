Heavy lift rigging in Pittsburgh, PA

A crane needs open sky, outrigger space, and ground that can take the bearing. Most heavy equipment in Pittsburgh sits where none of that exists — under a bay roof, in a pit, behind a column line, or in a building the load has to roll into. In Pittsburgh's steel & Marcellus energy equipment plants, the way a heavy piece moves is usually up a few inches, along a planned path, and down onto its set point.

Badass Logistics plans those lifts from the load data out: weight and center of gravity, the lift points the builder allows, floor capacity along the route, and the headroom at every stage. We set hydraulic gantries where the piece has to go up, jack and crib it where it has to come off a truck or a foundation, and skid or roll it where it has to travel. When there is room for a crane, the crane lift and the inside move go into one plan. Jacking and skidding, explained →

The buildings decide the method in Pittsburgh. The stock here is older multi-storey brick mill buildings in the river valleys with narrow street access, steep grades and limited crane clearance, which is why so many heavy pieces move under a gantry or on skid track rather than on a hook. The heaviest loads in the metro are steel rolling and forming equipment, CNC machining centers, welding and fabrication equipment, and material handling cranes — each one planned from its weight, lift points, and the floor under it.

When the equipment leaves Pittsburgh

Plenty of Pittsburgh jobs start and finish on one floor. The rest have to travel — to a new building across the metro, a sister plant down I-76, I-80, I-81 and I-95, or a facility in another state. We don't hand that part off. It runs as project freight inside the same plan: machines crated or prepped on site, loads sequenced to the rig-out, transport through our licensed broker and carrier partners with dedicated capacity for bigger moves, and our crew waiting at the destination to set it.

The heaviest pieces bound for Pittsburgh often arrive by rail or water before they ever reach a road. Served by CSX and Norfolk Southern, with Norfolk Southern's Mon Line running the length of the Monongahela River corridor. The Port of Pittsburgh spans more than 200 miles of navigable waterway across the Allegheny, Monongahela and Ohio rivers, moving bulk cargo including steel, coal and chemicals by barge. Getting a load off a railcar or a barge and onto a trailer is jacking and cribbing work in its own right, and it goes into the same plan as the set at the far end.

How we price a Pittsburgh heavy lift job

Every heavy lift rigging job in Pittsburgh, PA is quoted from the actual work, not a rate sheet. These are the things that move the number — send them with your request and you'll get a real quote back fast:

  • Load weight, dimensions, and center of gravity, and the lift points the builder allows
  • Headroom, door, and aisle clearances along the whole path
  • Floor capacity on the route and at the set point
  • Whether it comes off a trailer, out of a pit, or off a foundation
  • Setting tolerance — onto anchors, soleplates, or a new pad

Photos of the equipment, its nameplate, and the path out of the building save a site visit on smaller Pittsburgh jobs. For bigger moves we walk both sites before we price anything.

Pittsburgh Heavy Lift FAQ

Do you provide heavy lift rigging in Pittsburgh?
Yes — hydraulic gantry lifts, jacking and cribbing, and skidding and rolling throughout Pittsburgh, PA and the surrounding metro, serving its energy and petrochemical base. Get a quote →
When is a gantry or jacking better than a crane?
When there is a roof overhead, no space for outriggers, or the load has to travel inside the building after it is lifted. A gantry lifts and traverses in a bay, and jacks and skids move it the rest of the way. Where a crane can reach, we use one.
How much do heavy lift riggers cost in Pittsburgh?
There is no flat rate for heavy lift rigging — the price comes from the equipment, the access at both ends, the crew and gear, distance, and setting work. Send the equipment list and both sites and we'll quote your Pittsburgh job fast, usually the same day.
Will the floor take the load?
That gets checked before anything moves. Floor capacity along the whole path is part of the plan, and where it falls short we spread the load with mats, plates, or cribbing, or change the route. Floor loading for machinery →
Can you set equipment onto a new foundation?
Yes. We lower it onto the anchors or soleplates to the layout, and our millwrights can level and align it once it is down.
What do you jack and skid in Pittsburgh?
Most often steel rolling and forming equipment, CNC machining centers, welding and fabrication equipment, and material handling cranes, in the Mon Valley steel corridor along the Monongahela River, the Neville Island industrial district, and the Findlay Township industrial area near Pittsburgh International Airport and the plants around them.
