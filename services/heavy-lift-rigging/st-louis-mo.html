Heavy lift rigging in St. Louis, MO

A crane needs open sky, outrigger space, and ground that can take the bearing. Most heavy equipment in St. Louis sits where none of that exists — under a bay roof, in a pit, behind a column line, or in a building the load has to roll into. In St. Louis's manufacturing & Mississippi barge corridor plants, the way a heavy piece moves is usually up a few inches, along a planned path, and down onto its set point.

Badass Logistics plans those lifts from the load data out: weight and center of gravity, the lift points the builder allows, floor capacity along the route, and the headroom at every stage. We set hydraulic gantries where the piece has to go up, jack and crib it where it has to come off a truck or a foundation, and skid or roll it where it has to travel. When there is room for a crane, the crane lift and the inside move go into one plan. Jacking and skidding, explained →

The buildings decide the method in St. Louis. The stock here is large single-storey aerospace and automotive assembly plants alongside older multi-storey brick industrial buildings near the riverfront with tighter dock access, which is why so many heavy pieces move under a gantry or on skid track rather than on a hook. The heaviest loads in the metro are aircraft assembly jigs and tooling, stamping presses, brewing and packaging line equipment, chemical process vessels, and CNC machining centers — each one planned from its weight, lift points, and the floor under it.

When the equipment leaves St. Louis

Plenty of St. Louis jobs start and finish on one floor. The rest have to travel — to a new building across the metro, a sister plant down I-70, I-44, I-35 and I-29, or a facility in another state. We don't hand that part off. It runs as project freight inside the same plan: machines crated or prepped on site, loads sequenced to the rig-out, transport through our licensed broker and carrier partners with dedicated capacity for bigger moves, and our crew waiting at the destination to set it.

The heaviest pieces bound for St. Louis often arrive by rail or water before they ever reach a road. Getting a load off a railcar or a barge and onto a trailer is jacking and cribbing work in its own right, and it goes into the same plan as the set at the far end.

How we price a St. Louis heavy lift job

Every heavy lift rigging job in St. Louis, MO is quoted from the actual work, not a rate sheet. These are the things that move the number — send them with your request and you'll get a real quote back fast:

  • Load weight, dimensions, and center of gravity, and the lift points the builder allows
  • Headroom, door, and aisle clearances along the whole path
  • Floor capacity on the route and at the set point
  • Whether it comes off a trailer, out of a pit, or off a foundation
  • Setting tolerance — onto anchors, soleplates, or a new pad

Photos of the equipment, its nameplate, and the path out of the building save a site visit on smaller St. Louis jobs. For bigger moves we walk both sites before we price anything.

St. Louis Heavy Lift FAQ

Do you provide heavy lift rigging in St. Louis?
Yes — hydraulic gantry lifts, jacking and cribbing, and skidding and rolling throughout St. Louis, MO and the surrounding metro, serving its manufacturing base. Get a quote →
When is a gantry or jacking better than a crane?
When there is a roof overhead, no space for outriggers, or the load has to travel inside the building after it is lifted. A gantry lifts and traverses in a bay, and jacks and skids move it the rest of the way. Where a crane can reach, we use one.
How much do heavy lift riggers cost in St. Louis?
There is no flat rate for heavy lift rigging — the price comes from the equipment, the access at both ends, the crew and gear, distance, and setting work. Send the equipment list and both sites and we'll quote your St. Louis job fast, usually the same day.
Will the floor take the load?
That gets checked before anything moves. Floor capacity along the whole path is part of the plan, and where it falls short we spread the load with mats, plates, or cribbing, or change the route. Floor loading for machinery →
Can you set equipment onto a new foundation?
Yes. We lower it onto the anchors or soleplates to the layout, and our millwrights can level and align it once it is down.
What do you jack and skid in St. Louis?
Most often aircraft assembly jigs and tooling, stamping presses, brewing and packaging line equipment, chemical process vessels, and CNC machining centers, in the Earth City Industrial area near the Missouri River, America's Central Port and River's Edge Business Park in Granite City, Illinois, the Kelsey Business & Technology Park, and the St. Louis riverfront port district along the Mississippi and the plants around them.
