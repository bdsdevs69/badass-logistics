Heavy lift rigging in Kansas City, MO

A crane needs open sky, outrigger space, and ground that can take the bearing. Most heavy equipment in Kansas City sits where none of that exists — under a bay roof, in a pit, behind a column line, or in a building the load has to roll into. In Kansas City's rail & distribution crossroads plants, the way a heavy piece moves is usually up a few inches, along a planned path, and down onto its set point.

Badass Logistics plans those lifts from the load data out: weight and center of gravity, the lift points the builder allows, floor capacity along the route, and the headroom at every stage. We set hydraulic gantries where the piece has to go up, jack and crib it where it has to come off a truck or a foundation, and skid or roll it where it has to travel. When there is room for a crane, the crane lift and the inside move go into one plan. Jacking and skidding, explained →

The buildings decide the method in Kansas City. The stock here is large-footprint single-storey stamping and final-assembly plants in the automotive corridors alongside older multi-storey warehouse buildings in the historic West Bottoms district, which is why so many heavy pieces move under a gantry or on skid track rather than on a hook. The heaviest loads in the metro are stamping presses, robotic weld cells, engine and transmission machining lines, bulk grain handling and milling equipment, and biologics and animal health process equipment — each one planned from its weight, lift points, and the floor under it.

How we price a Kansas City heavy lift job

Every heavy lift rigging job in Kansas City, MO is quoted from the actual work, not a rate sheet. These are the things that move the number — send them with your request and you'll get a real quote back fast:

  • Load weight, dimensions, and center of gravity, and the lift points the builder allows
  • Headroom, door, and aisle clearances along the whole path
  • Floor capacity on the route and at the set point
  • Whether it comes off a trailer, out of a pit, or off a foundation
  • Setting tolerance — onto anchors, soleplates, or a new pad

Photos of the equipment, its nameplate, and the path out of the building save a site visit on smaller Kansas City jobs. For bigger moves we walk both sites before we price anything.

Kansas City Heavy Lift FAQ

Do you provide heavy lift rigging in Kansas City?
Yes — hydraulic gantry lifts, jacking and cribbing, and skidding and rolling throughout Kansas City, MO and the surrounding metro, serving its distribution and manufacturing base. Get a quote →
When is a gantry or jacking better than a crane?
When there is a roof overhead, no space for outriggers, or the load has to travel inside the building after it is lifted. A gantry lifts and traverses in a bay, and jacks and skids move it the rest of the way. Where a crane can reach, we use one.
How much do heavy lift riggers cost in Kansas City?
There is no flat rate for heavy lift rigging — the price comes from the equipment, the access at both ends, the crew and gear, distance, and setting work. Send the equipment list and both sites and we'll quote your Kansas City job fast, usually the same day.
Will the floor take the load?
That gets checked before anything moves. Floor capacity along the whole path is part of the plan, and where it falls short we spread the load with mats, plates, or cribbing, or change the route. Floor loading for machinery →
Can you set equipment onto a new foundation?
Yes. We lower it onto the anchors or soleplates to the layout, and our millwrights can level and align it once it is down.
What do you jack and skid in Kansas City?
Most often stamping presses, robotic weld cells, engine and transmission machining lines, bulk grain handling and milling equipment, and biologics and animal health process equipment, in the Ford Kansas City Assembly Plant complex in Claycomo, the GM Fairfax Assembly and Stamping Plant in Kansas City, Kansas, the Kansas City Animal Health Corridor, and the West Bottoms warehouse district and the plants around them.
