Machinery removal in Green Bay, WI

Removal is not junk hauling. A machine being sold has to reach its buyer working; one being scrapped still has to come out without damaging the building, the machines staying behind, or the people around it. Across Green Bay's paper & packaging machinery base, most removals are one of three things — a single machine being replaced, a line being retired, or a whole floor coming out for a closure or lease exit.

Badass Logistics coordinates lockout with your maintenance team or licensed trades, tags every asset, drains and secures what has to be drained, splits what won't fit the opening, and sequences the load-out so nothing blocks the dock. Resale units are prepped and loaded for the buyer, relocation units run as project freight through our licensed broker and carrier partners, and when the rest of the plant is moving too, it becomes a plant relocation.

When the equipment leaves Green Bay

Plenty of Green Bay jobs start and finish on one floor. The rest have to travel — to a new building across the metro, a sister plant down I-94, I-43 and I-90, or a facility in another state. We don't hand that part off. It runs as project freight inside the same plan: machines crated or prepped on site, loads sequenced to the rig-out, transport through our licensed broker and carrier partners with dedicated capacity for bigger moves, and our crew waiting at the destination to set it.

Where a removed machine goes decides how it leaves. Served by Canadian National, which reaches the Fox River terminal and mill district over former Wisconsin Central trackage. Resale units bound for another state or overseas are prepped and loaded for whichever routing the buyer uses, while scrap goes local — so a Green Bay decommissioning usually runs two or three load-out streams at once.

How we price a Green Bay removal job

Every machinery removal & decommissioning job in Green Bay, WI is quoted from the actual work, not a rate sheet. These are the things that move the number — send them with your request and you'll get a real quote back fast:

  • The asset list, weights, and which units are resale, relocation, or scrap
  • The route out: doors, aisles, docks, and whether anything has to be split
  • Lockout, draining, and disconnect coordination with your team or trades
  • The deadline — lease end, closure date, or the buyer's pickup window
  • Floor make-ready: anchors cut flush, pits covered, area left clean

Photos of the equipment, its nameplate, and the path out of the building save a site visit on smaller Green Bay jobs. For bigger moves we walk both sites before we price anything.

Green Bay Removal FAQ

Do you do machinery removal in Green Bay?
Yes — single machines, production lines, and full plant decommissioning throughout Green Bay, WI and the surrounding metro, serving its process-industry base. Get a quote →
Can you remove a machine we sold to a buyer?
Yes. We prep and protect it so it arrives working, then load it for the buyer's carrier or run the freight as part of the project through our licensed broker and carrier partners.
How much do machinery removal cost in Green Bay?
There is no flat rate for machinery removal & decommissioning — the price comes from the equipment, the access at both ends, the crew and gear, distance, and setting work. Send the equipment list and both sites and we'll quote your Green Bay job fast, usually the same day.
What if the machine won't fit through the door?
It gets split at factory joints where the builder allows it, or rigged out through a wall opening or the roof. The Green Bay site walk decides which, before anything is quoted.
Who disconnects the power and utilities?
Qualified personnel — your maintenance team or licensed trades. We coordinate the lockout sequence and don't touch the machine until it's verified safe.

Floors across the metro's pulp and paper manufacturing, corrugated packaging and paper converting, bulk-cargo terminal handling, and food and meat processing, in the Shipyard District along the west side of the Fox River, a legacy of the city's lumber, paper and maritime industries, the Fox River paper-mill corridor running through downtown and the west side, Grandview Industrial Park on the city's east side, and the Port of Green Bay terminal district at the mouth of the Fox River and the areas around them — single machines, retired lines, and full closures.
