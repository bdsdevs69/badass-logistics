How we price a Huntsville rigging job

Every industrial rigging job in Huntsville, AL is quoted from the actual work, not a rate sheet. These are the things that move the number — send them with your request and you'll get a real quote back fast:

  • The load: weight, dimensions, and center of gravity
  • The method: skates and jacks, gantry, forklift, or crane — and who supplies the crane
  • Site access, headroom, floor ratings, and any street or site closures
  • Crew size and schedule, including nights and weekends around production
  • Setting, leveling, and anchoring at the final position

Photos of the equipment, its nameplate, and the path out of the building save a site visit on smaller Huntsville jobs. For bigger moves we walk both sites before we price anything.

Do you offer rigging services in Huntsville?
Yes — industrial rigging, machine setting, and heavy lifts throughout Huntsville, AL and the surrounding metro, serving its aerospace and defense base. Single machines to full production lines. Get a quote →
How heavy a load can you rig in Huntsville?
From a few hundred pounds to 200,000 lbs and beyond. We size the gear — cranes, gantries, skates, and jack-and-slide — to the load and the site, and plan every pick before rig day.
How much do riggers cost in Huntsville?
There is no flat rate for industrial rigging — the price comes from the equipment, the access at both ends, the crew and gear, distance, and setting work. Send the equipment list and both sites and we'll quote your Huntsville job fast, usually the same day.
What kinds of rigging do you do in Huntsville?

Do you set and level the machine after the lift?
Yes — we set the load on its new pad or foundation and level it to the manufacturer's spec, ready for recommissioning.
Are you a rigging contractor or a machinery mover?
Both, and on a Huntsville job they are one scope rather than two vendors. Rigging contractors plan and execute the lift; machinery movers own the equipment end to end. Our riggers survey the job, size the gear, make the pick, then set and level the machine — so there is no handoff between a crane crew and whoever moves it afterward.
