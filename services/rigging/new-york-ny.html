How we price a New York rigging job

Every industrial rigging job in New York, NY is quoted from the actual work, not a rate sheet. These are the things that move the number — send them with your request and you'll get a real quote back fast:

  • The load: weight, dimensions, and center of gravity
  • The method: skates and jacks, gantry, forklift, or crane — and who supplies the crane
  • Site access, headroom, floor ratings, and any street or site closures
  • Crew size and schedule, including nights and weekends around production
  • Setting, leveling, and anchoring at the final position

Photos of the equipment, its nameplate, and the path out of the building save a site visit on smaller New York jobs. For bigger moves we walk both sites before we price anything.

questions

New York Rigging FAQ

Do you offer rigging services in New York?
Yes — industrial rigging, machine setting, and heavy lifts throughout New York, NY and the surrounding metro, serving its manufacturing base. Single machines to full production lines. Get a quote →
How heavy a load can you rig in New York?
From a few hundred pounds to 200,000 lbs and beyond. We size the gear — cranes, gantries, skates, and jack-and-slide — to the load and the site, and plan every pick before rig day.
How much do riggers cost in New York?
There is no flat rate for industrial rigging — the price comes from the equipment, the access at both ends, the crew and gear, distance, and setting work. Send the equipment list and both sites and we'll quote your New York job fast, usually the same day.
What kinds of rigging do you do in New York?
Machine setting, crane and gantry lifts, jacking and skidding, millwright installation, MRI and medical equipment, data center, chiller, and transformer rigging — see every type on our rigging page.
Do you set and level the machine after the lift?
Yes — we set the load on its new pad or foundation and level it to the manufacturer's spec, ready for recommissioning.
Are you a rigging contractor or a machinery mover?
Both, and on a New York job they are one scope rather than two vendors. Rigging contractors plan and execute the lift; machinery movers own the equipment end to end. Our riggers survey the job, size the gear, make the pick, then set and level the machine — so there is no handoff between a crane crew and whoever moves it afterward.
