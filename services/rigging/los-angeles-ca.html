Industrial rigging in Los Angeles, CA

Los Angeles's ports & industry base runs on machines that can't be muscled onto a truck — presses, machining centers, transformers, and production lines that have to come off the floor, through the door, and onto a trailer without a scratch. That's rigging: engineered lifts, air skates and gantries, and a crew that measures every doorway before anything moves.

Badass Logistics rigs it like an engineering problem and runs it like a road crew across Los Angeles. We plan the pick, protect the floors, and set the load to spec — and when the load has to leave the building, the same team runs the move and the project freight, so it gets rigged out, delivered, and reset without a hand-off.

Rigging in Los Angeles, CA is mostly a building problem, and in this metro the buildings are in the Vernon industrial district south of downtown, the City of Industry business park in the San Gabriel Valley, the Alameda Corridor rail belt through Commerce and Carson, and the harbor-area industrial district near the ports. They are older multi-tenant industrial buildings in Vernon and Commerce with tight column spacing, low dock heights and narrow street access built long before modern trailer traffic — column spacing, door height and floor rating decide the gear long before the weight does. The lifts themselves follow the metro's apparel and textile manufacturing, aerospace component manufacturing, food and beverage processing, and furniture and cabinetry manufacturing.

When the equipment leaves Los Angeles

Plenty of Los Angeles jobs start and finish on one floor. The rest have to travel — to a new building across the metro, a sister plant down I-5, I-10, I-15 and I-80, or a facility in another state. We don't hand that part off. It runs as project freight inside the same plan: machines crated or prepped on site, loads sequenced to the rig-out, transport through our licensed broker and carrier partners with dedicated capacity for bigger moves, and our crew waiting at the destination to set it.

Not every Los Angeles rig finishes on the floor it started on. The adjoining Port of Los Angeles and Port of Long Beach form the busiest container port complex in the country, handling containerized and breakbulk cargo. Add I-5, I-10, I-15 and I-80 for the road legs and a machine rigged out of a Los Angeles plant can leave on a trailer, in a rail car, or over water — the rigging plan is built backwards from whichever it is.

How we price a Los Angeles rigging job

Every industrial rigging job in Los Angeles, CA is quoted from the actual work, not a rate sheet. These are the things that move the number — send them with your request and you'll get a real quote back fast:

  • The load: weight, dimensions, and center of gravity
  • The method: skates and jacks, gantry, forklift, or crane — and who supplies the crane
  • Site access, headroom, floor ratings, and any street or site closures
  • Crew size and schedule, including nights and weekends around production
  • Setting, leveling, and anchoring at the final position

Photos of the equipment, its nameplate, and the path out of the building save a site visit on smaller Los Angeles jobs. For bigger moves we walk both sites before we price anything.

Los Angeles Rigging FAQ

Do you offer rigging services in Los Angeles?
Yes — industrial rigging, machine setting, and heavy lifts throughout Los Angeles, CA and the surrounding metro, serving its distribution and manufacturing base. Single machines to full production lines. Get a quote →
How heavy a load can you rig in Los Angeles?
From a few hundred pounds to 200,000 lbs and beyond. We size the gear — cranes, gantries, skates, and jack-and-slide — to the load and the site, and plan every pick before rig day.
How much do riggers cost in Los Angeles?
There is no flat rate for industrial rigging — the price comes from the equipment, the access at both ends, the crew and gear, distance, and setting work. Send the equipment list and both sites and we'll quote your Los Angeles job fast, usually the same day.
What kinds of rigging do you do in Los Angeles?
Machine setting, crane and gantry lifts, jacking and skidding, millwright installation, MRI and medical equipment, data center, chiller, and transformer rigging — see every type on our rigging page.
Do you set and level the machine after the lift?
Yes — we set the load on its new pad or foundation and level it to the manufacturer's spec, ready for recommissioning.
Are you a rigging contractor or a machinery mover?
Both, and on a Los Angeles job they are one scope rather than two vendors. Rigging contractors plan and execute the lift; machinery movers own the equipment end to end. Our riggers survey the job, size the gear, make the pick, then set and level the machine — so there is no handoff between a crane crew and whoever moves it afterward.
