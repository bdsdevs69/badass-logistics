Quick answer: Industrial rigging is the planning and hands-on work of lifting, moving, and setting heavy equipment — machines, production lines, MRI scanners, transformers, chillers — often through tight doorways or onto elevated pads. Badass Logistics is a rigging company first: our own crews and rigging gear handle every kind of rigging nationwide, from a single machine to a full plant, set and leveled to spec.

Every kind of rigging, one accountable crew

Rigging is not one job. Setting a 40,000-lb press on a new foundation, skating a CT scanner down a hospital corridor, craning a chiller onto a roof, and tearing out a production line are four different problems with four different gear lists. What they share is the part that matters: somebody has to measure the load, find the center of gravity, check the floor, and plan the pick before anything leaves the ground.

That's the work Badass Logistics is built around. We own the rigging side — the crews, the jacks, skates, gantries and slings, and the planning — and we run it the same way on every job: survey first, documented lift plan, controlled execution, then set, level, and sign off. One crew from the first measurement to the final shim, so nothing falls into the gap between a rigger, a millwright, and whoever was supposed to move it next.

When a rigged machine has to travel to another building, another city, or another state, we don't hand you off. We manage it as project freight — crated, loaded, and moved on licensed partner carriers inside the same project plan — and our crew is waiting at the other end to set it.

More kinds of rigging we handle

Riggers get called for far more than machine shops. If it's heavy, awkward, fragile, or sitting somewhere it was never meant to leave, it's a rigging job. A few more we run regularly:

Precision & laboratory equipment

Semiconductor tools, electron microscopes, lab instruments, and test stands that can't take shock or tilt — moved on air-ride with vibration-conscious handling and set on isolated pads.

Printing presses & packaging lines

Multi-unit presses, folders, converting and packaging lines broken down in sequence, labeled, moved, and reassembled in order.

Tanks, vessels & process equipment

Storage tanks, pressure vessels, reactors, and skids rigged out of process buildings and set on new saddles or foundations.

Rooftop & crane-in work

Rooftop units, cooling towers, and mechanical equipment picked onto roofs and into penthouses, with the lift, the street closure, and the setting planned as one job.

Structural & equipment setting

Precast pieces, steel members, modular skids, and enclosures set into place on new construction and plant expansions.

Conveyors, racking & warehouse equipment

Conveyor systems, pallet racking, mezzanines, and automation cells removed, relocated, and reinstalled around live operations.

Food, beverage & pharma lines

Processing, filling, and packaging lines moved with sanitary handling, sequenced so the new room is ready for validation.

Aerospace, defense & test equipment

Autoclaves, test cells, large machining centers, and tooling moved with the documentation and care those programs expect.

by the book

Safe, planned, documented

Every lift runs on a written plan — dimensions, weights, rigging geometry, equipment selection, and sequence settled before anyone touches the load. Our crews work to OSHA rigging and signaling requirements, every piece of rigging hardware is load-rated and inspected before the lift, and floor loading gets checked along the entire path of travel, not just under the pick.

No improvised rigging, no guessing at weights off a brochure, no "we'll figure it out on site." If the numbers don't work on paper, the lift doesn't happen until they do.

after the lift

When the machine has to go somewhere

Plenty of rigging jobs start and end in the same building. The rest need freight: a machine rigged out of one plant, crated, trucked across the country, and set in another. Most companies split that into a rigger on each end and a carrier nobody's watching in the middle.

We run it as one project. The same team that rigs the machine out manages the crating, the load-out, the transport on licensed partner carriers, and the rig-in at the destination — one schedule, one point of contact, one crew accountable for the machine the whole way.

Rigging FAQ

What is industrial rigging?
It's the planning and physical work of lifting, moving, and setting heavy equipment — using cranes, gantries, jacks, skates, and slings to relocate loads safely and precisely, often into tight or elevated spaces.
What types of rigging do you do?
Every kind: machinery moving, plant relocation, MRI and medical equipment, crane lifts, jacking and skidding, millwright installation, data center, chiller and HVAC, and transformer and generator rigging — plus lab, press, tank, and rooftop work.
How heavy a load can you rig?
From a single crate to machinery over 200,000 lbs. Every lift is matched to load-rated rigging and the right equipment for the weight, the dimensions, and the site.
Can you move the equipment to another facility too?
Yes. We manage it as project freight — crating, loading, transport on licensed partner carriers, and rig-in at the destination — so one team owns the machine the whole way.
Do you work nights and weekends?
Yes. Most rigging is scheduled around production, so off-shift and weekend moves are normal for us.
