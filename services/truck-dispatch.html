Why we only dispatch fleets

Plenty of dispatch services will take anybody with a truck and an MC number. We don't. Our desk is built around fleets of four power units and up, because that's where dispatch actually works as a system: lanes can be planned across trucks, reloads can be lined up before a truck empties, and the freight coming out of our own projects has somewhere reliable to go.

Here's the part most dispatch services can't offer. Badass Logistics is a rigging and project freight company first. We have offices, relationships with shippers and manufacturers, and freight moving through our network every week — machines between plants, crated equipment to job sites, dedicated lanes that run for months. When your trucks are on our desk, that freight is available to cover them, alongside loads we source from direct shippers, brokers we've vetted, and the boards.

You keep your own authority, your own insurance, and your own drivers. You approve every load before it's booked. No forced dispatch, no pressure to run a lane that doesn't work for your equipment or your drivers' home time.

who it's for

Is fleet dispatch a fit for your company?

We're a good fit if you run a trucking company with at least four trucks under your own authority, your drivers are employees or leased to your company, and you want more of your freight coming from steady sources instead of the board every afternoon.

Where we're a weaker fit: drivers leased onto another carrier who want their own dispatcher, and anyone looking for someone to push trucks onto whatever pays the highest number today with no plan for tomorrow. If you're running one or two trucks now and building toward a fleet, tell us where you're headed and we'll tell you straight whether the timing works.

  • Minimum four power units running under your authority
  • Active insurance and authority documents we can keep on file for setups
  • Dry van, reefer, flatbed, step deck, conestoga, or power only
  • A fleet owner or manager who wants lanes planned, not just loads found

day to day

What a dispatch service actually does, hour by hour

Dispatching for trucking companies isn't one job, it's several running at once across every truck on the desk. Here's what a day looks like when it's working:

Reading the board before anyone else does

Every morning starts with knowing exactly where each truck sits: which ones are empty, which ones will be by end of day, and which ones are already booked into tomorrow. That picture is what turns load sourcing into planning instead of scrambling.

Sourcing and negotiating each load

Freight gets pulled from our own rigging and project network first, then direct shippers and vetted brokers, then the boards if a truck still needs covering. Every rate gets negotiated against the lane's history, the deadhead on either end, and what the freight actually requires — a flatbed load needing tarps and straps isn't priced the same way a dry van load is.

Keeping drivers in the loop

A dispatcher who only talks to a driver at pickup and delivery isn't doing the job. Ours stay in contact through the run — appointment changes, detour warnings, a heads-up when a receiver is running behind — so a driver isn't finding out about a problem from the shipper before dispatch tells them.

Building lanes that hold

A single booked load is a transaction. A lane is a plan: the same truck running the same corridor on a repeatable cycle because the freight on both ends is reliable. Our dedicated lanes come out of exactly this — project freight that runs often enough to build a lane around, handed to a fleet on our desk instead of re-sourced from scratch every week.

the back office

The work that eats a fleet manager's day

Finding the load is maybe a third of dispatch. The rest is paperwork and follow-through — and on a fleet, that work multiplies by every truck. Our desk takes it on:

Setup and compliance documents

Certificates of insurance, W-9s, authority letters, and carrier packets kept current and sent out for new broker and shipper setups, so a good load isn't lost to a missing form.

In-transit follow-through

Check calls, tracking updates, and appointment changes handled with the receiver and the customer. When a truck sits at a dock, detention and layover are documented as they happen and pursued afterward. See how dispatchers and brokers differ in our guide to dispatcher vs freight broker.

Getting paid

Signed BOLs and PODs collected, invoice packets assembled, and submissions sent to your factoring company or directly to the customer — so cash doesn't stall because a document is still in a driver's cab. Fee structures differ by provider; our guide to how truck dispatchers get paid walks through the common models before you sign with anyone.

Hours and home time

Loads are planned around each driver's available hours and home-time commitments. A load that looks great but blows up a driver's weekend isn't a good load for your fleet.

flatbed dispatch

Flatbed dispatch, and what to keep in-house

A dry van dispatcher and a flatbed dispatcher aren't doing the same job. Flatbed, step deck, and conestoga freight brings trailer questions the desk has to plan around before a load ever gets booked.

Matching freight to the right trailer

Deck height, tarping, and securement change which loads a truck can even take, and pricing them wrong eats the margin. If you're deciding what to add to the fleet or want your drivers thinking about it the same way dispatch does, our guides on step deck vs drop deck trailers and loading and securing a conestoga trailer cover it, and the trailer selector tool matches a load's dimensions to the trailer type before you commit a truck to it.

What stays with you

Dispatch runs the loads, the rates, and the paperwork. It doesn't run your company. Hiring and managing drivers, maintaining the trucks, your safety and compliance program, and any direct customer accounts you already have all stay yours — the desk plugs into that, it doesn't replace it.

How to judge a dispatch service before you sign

Ask exactly this: do you keep your own authority and insurance, do you approve every load or is it forced dispatch, where does the freight actually come from versus just the boards, how often will you hear from your dispatcher during a run, and how are detention, TONU, and claims handled when something goes wrong. A dispatch service that can't answer those plainly on the first call isn't one to hand a fleet to.

Fleet dispatch FAQ

How many trucks do you need to run to work with us?
The desk is built around fleets of roughly four power units and up, because planning reloads across several trucks is what makes it work. Smaller than that and the economics get thin — but if you're growing toward it, tell us your plan and we'll say honestly whether now is the right time.
What does a truck dispatch service do for a fleet?
Finds and books freight, plans reloads across trucks, negotiates rates, handles broker and shipper setups and rate cons, runs check calls, pursues detention and TONU, and builds billing and factoring packets.
Where does the freight come from?
From our own rigging and project freight network, direct shippers, vetted brokers, and load boards — matched to your equipment and lanes.
Do you dispatch flatbed, step deck, and conestoga fleets?
Yes. Flatbed freight adds trailer and securement questions the desk plans around before a load is booked — see our guides on step deck vs drop deck trailers and loading a conestoga, or run a load through the trailer selector.
How do truck dispatchers get paid?
Pay structures differ by provider and by fleet size — our guide on how truck dispatchers get paid covers the common models. Ours is set on the onboarding call once we know your fleet and equipment, not before.
Is it forced dispatch?
No. You keep your authority and approve every load before it's booked.
What should stay in-house if I hire a dispatch service?
Hiring and managing drivers, maintaining your trucks, running your safety and compliance program, and any direct customer accounts you already have. Dispatch runs the loads, rates, and paperwork — it plugs into your company, it doesn't replace it.
How much does fleet dispatch cost?
Pricing is set on the onboarding call based on fleet size, equipment, and how much of the back office you want us to run. No numbers until we know your fleet.
How do I get started?
Apply for fleet dispatch with your fleet size, equipment, and home base. We'll set up an onboarding call.
