Printing press movers in Chicago, IL

Moving a press is mostly a sequencing problem. Each printing unit, the feeder, the delivery, the dryers and the controls come apart in an order, travel as separate loads, and have to reach the new floor in the order they go back down. In Chicago, print and packaging shops run tight schedules, so the downtime window is usually what the whole plan is built around.

Badass Logistics works alongside your press technicians or the manufacturer's: they handle the mechanical and electrical disconnects, and we rig, skid, crate, and load each section. At the new site we set the units on the layout and our millwrights level and align them for the technicians to reconnect. Moving the whole plant? That's plant relocation. When the equipment leaves Chicago

Plenty of Chicago jobs start and finish on one floor. The rest have to travel — to a new building across the metro, a sister plant down I-55, I-80, I-90 and I-94, or a facility in another state. We don't hand that part off. It runs as project freight inside the same plan: machines crated or prepped on site, loads sequenced to the rig-out, transport through our licensed broker and carrier partners with dedicated capacity for bigger moves, and our crew waiting at the destination to set it.

Presses moving into or out of Chicago rarely travel as one load. The Illinois International Port District on Lake Calumet handles bulk and breakbulk cargo with access to the Great Lakes and, via the Illinois Waterway, the Mississippi River system. Served by BNSF, Union Pacific, CSX, Norfolk Southern, Canadian National and CPKC, with Chicago functioning as the largest rail interchange hub in North America. A used press sold overseas or bought in from another plant is crated unit by unit and sequenced so the sections reach the floor in the order they go back down.

How we price a Chicago press move job

Every printing press moving job in Chicago, IL is quoted from the actual work, not a rate sheet. These are the things that move the number — send them with your request and you'll get a real quote back fast:

  • Press make, model, number of units, and the bindery and finishing equipment
  • Who handles the teardown — in-house technicians or the manufacturer
  • Access at both sites: doors, docks, and floor
  • The downtime window and the restart date
  • Crating, staging, and the order the loads arrive in

Photos of the equipment, its nameplate, and the path out of the building save a site visit on smaller Chicago jobs. For bigger moves we walk both sites before we price anything.
