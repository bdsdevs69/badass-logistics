Machine-tool moving, done like the precision job it is

The fastest way to ruin a machine tool is to move it like furniture. Lift from the wrong casting point, leave an axis free to slide, or skip the re-level, and the machine powers on fine and never holds tolerance again. Badass Logistics treats every CNC move as an engineering job: a documented plan, lifting only from the manufacturer's designated points, and a machine that's squared back to spec before we hand it over.

From a single VMC across the shop to a full CNC shop relocation across state lines, you get one accountable crew. Our riggers handle the disconnect coordination, rig-out, and set. When the machine is headed to another building, it runs as project freight — crated or wrapped, moved on air-ride partner equipment, and met at the other end by the same crew.

The goal is simple: the machine should arrive as straight as it left, and your programmers shouldn't be chasing a new geometry problem the week after the move.

why it's different

Lift points, not pry bars

CNC machines have designated lift and jacking points in the manual — and only those. Pry under the sheet metal or sling the casting wrong and you twist the machine's geometry. We pull the lift data first, protect the ways, lock the axes and spindle in their transport positions with the builder's shipping brackets where they exist, and move on toe jacks, machine skates, or air casters so the casting stays supported the whole way.

Weight concentration matters too. A machining center puts a lot of load on a small footprint, and older shop floors, trench covers, and dock plates aren't always rated for it. The survey checks the path, not just the pad.

between shops

Moving a CNC machine to another building

When a machine leaves the building, the risks change. Vibration and shock over hundreds of miles, moisture on unprotected ways, and securement that shifts under braking all show up as problems at power-on. We plan that stretch like the rest of the move:

  • Coolant, lube, and hydraulic reservoirs drained or secured per the builder
  • Ways and exposed surfaces cleaned, oiled, and wrapped against moisture
  • Crated or skidded when the machine or its components call for it
  • Air-ride equipment through our licensed carrier partners, with securement to real tie-down points
  • Delivery timed so the rigging crew is on site when the truck arrives

At the new shop the machine is set on its pad, anchored if required, and leveled to the builder's spec, ready for your service tech or the OEM to run their checks and first cut. See our guide to moving a CNC machine for the full breakdown.

How a CNC move runs

  1. Survey & lift dataBoth floors measured; weights, lift points, and floor loading pulled from the manual — not guessed.
  2. Power-down & secureQualified disconnect, coolant and air drained, axes and spindle locked with OEM bracing.
  3. Rig outToe jacks, machine skates or air casters, gantry or crane from the rated points on a planned path.
  4. Air-ride moveWrapped or crated and moved as project freight on air-ride partner equipment.
  5. Set, level & squarePlaced on the new pad and leveled to the builder's spec, ready for ramp-up and first cut.

questions

CNC machine moving FAQ

How much does it cost to move a CNC machine?
It depends on machine weight and class, rigging access at both ends, distance, and how much disconnect and re-level work is involved. Our guide on what drives the cost breaks it down; send the model and both sites for a real quote.
Do you re-level the machine after the move?
Always. It's set on the new pad and leveled to the builder's spec before hand-off, ready for your service tech or the OEM to verify.
Can you move a CNC machine to another state?
Yes. We rig it out, wrap or crate it, move it on air-ride as project freight through our licensed broker and carrier partners, and set it at the new shop.
Should the OEM be involved in the move?
For many machines, yes — especially for disconnect, shipping brackets, and post-move calibration. We coordinate with your OEM or service tech so each side knows exactly where their work starts.
Can you move a whole machine shop?
Yes. Full shops are sequenced machine by machine so the critical spindles come back online first. See plant relocation.
Do you move lathes and grinders too?
Yes — lathes, turning centers, grinders, EDM, press brakes, and lasers. See how to move a lathe without twisting the bed.
