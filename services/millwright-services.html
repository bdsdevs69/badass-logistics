Millwright & Machine Installation

Getting a machine onto its pad is rigging. Getting it to run right is millwright work: setting it on the anchors, leveling it to the builder's spec, aligning what drives what, and putting the line back together in order. We do both, so the crew that moved it is the crew that makes it ready to run.

Quick answer: Millwright services cover the installation and alignment of industrial machinery — setting equipment on foundations, anchoring, precision leveling, shaft and coupling alignment, and assembling conveyors and production lines. Badass Logistics provides millwright work alongside our rigging, so machines are moved, set, leveled, and aligned by one crew and handed off ready for utilities, commissioning, and start-up.

Where rigging ends and millwright work begins

A rigger's job traditionally ends when the load is on the ground in the right spot. A millwright's job starts there: making sure the machine sits on its foundation the way its builder intended, that it's level and not twisted, that the motor lines up with the pump or gearbox it drives, and that the conveyor feeding it runs true. Skip that part and a machine that was moved perfectly can still wear out bearings, chatter, or never hold tolerance.

On most projects those are two different contractors, and the machine sits in the gap between them. We close it. Our crews rig the equipment in, set it on its anchors, and handle the leveling, alignment, and mechanical reassembly — so the handoff to your electricians, pipefitters, or OEM start-up tech is clean.

Millwright work is a core part of our machinery moving and plant relocation jobs, and it stands on its own for new equipment installs, line reconfigurations, and removals. For more background, see what a millwright does.

why it matters

What bad installation costs later

Installation problems rarely show up on day one. They show up over months, as symptoms that get blamed on something else:

  • A machine tool that won't hold tolerance because the base is twisted, not level
  • Bearing and seal failures from misaligned couplings between motors and driven equipment
  • "Soft foot" — a machine foot that isn't fully supported — distorting the frame when anchors are tightened
  • Conveyors that track badly and jam at transfers because sections weren't set to a common line and elevation
  • Vibration that loosens anchors and fasteners over time

Every one of those is cheaper to prevent during installation than to chase after start-up. That's why leveling and alignment get their own time in our schedule instead of being squeezed into the last hour of a move. Our guide to machine leveling and alignment goes deeper.

Who does what on an install

Clear scope lines make installs go faster. On a typical job, here's how the work divides:

Our crew

Rigging the equipment in, setting it, shimming and anchoring, leveling, mechanical alignment, conveyor and line assembly, and mechanical reassembly of components removed for the move.

Your trades or the OEM

Foundation design and concrete, grouting where specified, electrical and controls connections, piping, and the builder's own start-up, calibration, and warranty checks.

Planned together

Anchor layouts, foundation readiness, and the sequence of who is on the floor when — so trades aren't stacked up waiting on each other.

questions

Millwright services FAQ

What does a millwright do?
Installs, sets, levels, aligns, and assembles industrial machinery and conveyors, and dismantles it for moves. Riggers move the load; millwrights make sure it runs right once it's there.
Do you install new equipment, or only machines you moved?
Both. We install new equipment arriving from the builder, including receiving it through our project freight service, as well as machines we relocate.
Do you do grouting and electrical connections?
Grouting, electrical, controls, and piping are usually handled by specialized trades or the OEM. We coordinate the sequence with them and set and level the equipment ready for their work.
How level does a machine need to be?
It depends on the machine. The builder's installation manual sets the spec, and precision machine tools are far tighter than general equipment. We level to the spec provided.
Can you reassemble a production line after a move?
Yes. Lines are tagged, mapped, and photographed during teardown, then reassembled in order and aligned. See plant relocation.
