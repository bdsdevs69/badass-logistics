Transformer & Generator Moving & Rigging

Transformers and generators are some of the heaviest, most valuable, and least forgiving equipment on any site. A transformer that takes a hard jolt may need testing before it can be energized. We rig them off the truck and onto their foundations with jacks, skid systems, and cranes, planned around the OEM's handling requirements.

Quick answer: Transformer and generator rigging is the lifting, moving, and setting of power equipment — substation and pad-mount transformers, generator sets, switchgear, and turbines — onto pads and foundations. Badass Logistics uses hydraulic jacking and skidding, gantries, and crane lifts, rigged only from the manufacturer's designated jacking pads and lift points, with impact monitoring and OEM coordination from delivery to final set.

Power equipment has no margin for a rough set

A large transformer isn't just heavy. Its core and windings are sensitive to shock, it may ship with an impact recorder that the manufacturer will read before accepting it, and it can only be jacked and lifted from specific points built for the purpose. Lift from the tank wall or jack from the wrong spot and the damage may not show until testing — or worse, until it's energized.

Generators and switchgear bring their own problems: long enclosures with off-center weight, shipping splits that have to line up perfectly, and pads in tight equipment yards surrounded by things you don't want to hit. Our crews plan every set from the manufacturer's drawings, weights, and handling instructions, and choose the method that puts the least stress on the equipment and the site.

Many of these jobs don't use a crane at all. A transformer is often rolled off the trailer onto cribbing and jacked and skidded onto its pad. Generators and enclosures in open yards are usually crane lifts. Either way, the same crew runs the plan from the truck to the foundation. For the transport side, see how to transport a transformer.

the rules

How transformers and generators get handled

Every manufacturer publishes handling requirements for its equipment. Our plans follow them, and they usually come down to a few principles:

  • Jack and lift only from the designated jacking pads and lifting lugs, never from the tank walls, radiators, or bushings
  • Keep tilt within the manufacturer's limits and avoid shock loads during jacking, skidding, and setting
  • Leave impact recorders in place and undisturbed until the OEM or owner reviews them
  • Protect bushings, radiators, and accessories — often shipped separately and installed after setting
  • Support long enclosures and gensets at their designed support points to avoid frame distortion

Oil filling, nitrogen handling, dressing, testing, and electrical connection are specialized work done by the manufacturer, your electrical contractor, or testing firms. We coordinate the sequence so the equipment is set and ready when they arrive.

the methods

Jacking, skidding, or crane

The method depends on the equipment, the site, and how close the truck can get to the foundation:

Jack and skid

The equipment is jacked up from the trailer, transferred onto cribbing and skid track, and pushed across to the foundation in controlled strokes. Common for large transformers and tight substations.

Crane lift

Generators, enclosures, and smaller transformers in open yards are often picked directly from the truck to the pad with a planned crane lift.

Gantry

Where there's no room for a crane but the equipment has to be lifted and traveled, hydraulic gantries do the job with precise control.

questions

Transformer & generator rigging FAQ

How do you move a transformer onto its pad?
Most often by jacking it from the trailer onto cribbing and skid track, then skidding it across to the foundation. In open yards with good access, a crane lift can set it directly.
Why can't you lift a transformer from anywhere?
Transformers are designed to be lifted and jacked only at specific lugs and pads. Other parts of the tank and accessories aren't built to carry the load and can be damaged.
What is an impact recorder?
A device shipped on many transformers that records shocks during transport and handling. The manufacturer or owner reviews it to confirm the unit wasn't subjected to excessive impacts.
Do you set generators for data centers and hospitals?
Yes — standby generators and their enclosures on pads, in yards, and on rooftops. See data center rigging.
Do you fill transformers with oil or do testing?
No. Oil filling, nitrogen handling, dressing, testing, and connections are done by the manufacturer or specialized contractors. We set the equipment ready for their work.
