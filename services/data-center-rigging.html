Data Center Rigging

A data center build is a parade of heavy, expensive, schedule-critical equipment: generators, UPS modules, switchgear, cooling units, and racks that show up loaded. We rig it off the truck, through the building, and onto its pad or floor — without cracking a raised floor or taking down the hall next door.

Quick answer: Data center rigging is the specialized moving and setting of critical infrastructure — generators, UPS modules and battery cabinets, switchgear, PDUs, CRAH and chiller units, and loaded server racks — in new builds and operating facilities. Badass Logistics plans each move around floor loading, clean handling, and live-facility rules, then rigs, sets, and levels the equipment on schedule with the build.

Heavy equipment in a building that can't go down

Data center equipment is heavy in ways that surprise people. A UPS module or battery cabinet can concentrate thousands of pounds on a small footprint. Switchgear arrives in long shipping splits. A loaded rack can weigh far more than the floor tile it has to roll across. And all of it has to go into a building where dust, vibration, and an unplanned power event are unacceptable — often right next to halls that are already live.

We plan data center moves around those constraints. Every piece is checked for weight and footprint against the path it takes: dock levelers, corridors, freight elevators, slab-on-grade, and raised floor. Paths get plated where they need it. Equipment is handled clean. Moves in operating facilities are scheduled with the site's change windows and escort rules, not around them.

On new builds, the challenge is sequence. Equipment arrives in phases as rooms become ready, and a generator that lands before its pad is cured is a problem. Our project freight team can receive and stage equipment, and our rigging crews set it as each space is released.

What makes data center rigging different

The rigging methods are the same ones we use everywhere. The rules around them are stricter:

Floor loading

Raised floor systems, tiles, and elevated slabs have load ratings that concentrated equipment can exceed. We check ratings along the path and plate or bridge where the load needs to be spread.

Clean handling

Packaging is removed in designated areas, wheels and skates are clean, and debris doesn't travel into white space.

Live facility rules

In operating data centers, moves follow change-management windows, security escorts, and restrictions on work near energized equipment.

Tight schedules

Build schedules stack trades on top of each other. Rigging is planned with the general contractor so equipment is set when the room is ready and out of the way when it's not.

Generator yards, roofs, and pads

A lot of data center rigging happens outside: generators into yards, chillers and condensers onto roofs, transformers onto pads, and prefab skids onto foundations. Those are crane lifts or jacking and skidding jobs, planned with ground bearing, pad readiness, and the delivery sequence.

When equipment is coming from multiple manufacturers on different timelines, we can manage it as project freight — receiving and staging equipment off site and delivering it on the day the crew and the space are ready.

questions

Data center rigging FAQ

Can you move equipment in a live data center?
Yes. Moves in operating facilities are planned within the site's change windows, escort requirements, and restrictions near energized equipment, with clean handling throughout.
How do you move heavy equipment across a raised floor?
We check the floor system's ratings along the path and use plating, bridging, or load-spreading protection where the equipment's concentrated load requires it.
Do you set generators and switchgear?
Yes — generator sets on pads and in yards, switchgear shipping splits set and aligned, and UPS and battery cabinets moved into electrical rooms, ready for electrical trades.
Can you receive and stage equipment before the room is ready?
Yes. Equipment can be received, staged, and delivered in phases through our project freight service.
Do you move loaded server racks?
Yes, with attention to the rack's loaded weight, the floor rating, and how the rack is supported during the move. Some racks should be unloaded first; we'll flag that during planning.
