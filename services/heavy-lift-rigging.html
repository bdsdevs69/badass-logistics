Jacking, Skidding & Gantry Lifts

Some of the heaviest moves we make never see a crane. Inside a plant, under a low ceiling, or down a corridor, heavy equipment gets raised on hydraulic jacks, lifted with a gantry, and moved on skid track, skates, and rollers — controlled an inch at a time.

Quick answer: Jacking, skidding, and gantry lifts are rigging methods for moving very heavy equipment without a crane. Hydraulic jacks and cribbing raise the load, hydraulic gantries lift and travel it on track, and jack-and-slide skidding systems push it horizontally in controlled strokes. Badass Logistics uses these methods to move machines, transformers, vessels, and presses inside buildings, under low headroom, and where cranes can't go.

When a crane isn't the right tool

A crane needs room: a place to set up, ground that can take the outrigger loads, and a clear path through the air. Inside a plant, a substation, a basement mechanical room, or a hospital, you often have none of that. What you have is a heavy load, a slab, a doorway, and a few inches of headroom.

That's where the old-school heavy rigging methods earn their keep. Hydraulic jacks and cribbing raise a load in controlled lifts. Gantry systems pick it and travel it along track. Jack-and-slide skidding systems push it sideways in short strokes across a prepared path. Toe jacks, air skates, and roller dollies take it the rest of the way. None of it is fast, and that's the point: every inch is controlled.

These methods sit at the heart of our rigging work. They're how transformers get set on pads, presses get skidded into pits, MRI magnets get walked into shielded rooms, and big machines get moved across plants that were never built to let them out.

how the methods fit together

Lift, move, set — without a hook

Most heavy moves combine several methods in sequence. A typical indoor move might look like this:

Raise the load

Toe jacks or hydraulic jacks lift the equipment in small increments, with cribbing built up underneath at every stage so the load is never riding on the jacks alone.

Get it on something that moves

Skates, rollers, or a skid shoe go under the load at its support points, and it's lowered onto them evenly.

Travel it

The load moves on skates across plated floor, along a skid track under a push-pull system, or hangs from a gantry traveling on rails — whichever the weight, headroom, and path allow.

Set it

At the destination the process reverses: jack up, remove the rolling gear, lower onto the foundation or anchors, and level and align.

the engineering

What gets checked before the first stroke

Heavy lift rigging concentrates enormous weight onto small contact points. That makes the planning more important, not less:

  • Load weight and center of gravity, so jacking points share the load the way you expect
  • Floor and slab capacity along the full path, including trenches, joints, and elevated floors
  • Headroom for the gantry or jacking height, including overhead piping and bus duct
  • Support points on the equipment that are designed to carry load
  • Track, plate, and cribbing layout, and how the load is stopped and held at every stage
  • Synchronization of multi-point jacking and gantry legs so the load stays level

When the load leaves the building afterward, the same crew plans its project freight and meets it at the other end.

how it works

How a heavy lift job runs

  1. Survey the load & pathWeight, CG, support points, floor capacity, headroom, and the route from pick to set.
  2. Choose the methodGantry, jack-and-slide, jacking and cribbing, skates — or a combination — matched to the load and the space.
  3. Prep the pathSteel plate, track, and cribbing laid out; obstructions cleared; stop points marked.
  4. Lift & travelThe load is raised, moved, and held in controlled stages under a single lead rigger.
  5. Set & levelLowered onto its foundation or anchors and leveled, ready for connection.

Jacking & skidding FAQ

What is jacking and skidding?
A heavy rigging method where hydraulic jacks raise the load onto skid shoes or track, and push-pull skidding systems move it horizontally in controlled strokes. It's used for very heavy loads where cranes can't work.
What is a hydraulic gantry used for?
Lifting and traveling heavy equipment on rails inside buildings or tight sites — setting machines, transformers, and vessels where a crane can't set up or reach.
When is a gantry better than a crane?
Indoors, under low headroom, when the setup area is too small for a crane, or when the load only needs to be lifted and moved a short distance with very precise control.
Can you move a heavy machine across a plant floor?
Yes — on skates, rollers, or skid track over steel plate, after checking floor capacity along the path. See machinery moving.
How heavy can jacking and skidding move?
The methods scale with the gear and the planning. We rig loads from a few hundred pounds to 200,000 lbs and beyond, and every job is sized from the actual load, support points, and floor.
