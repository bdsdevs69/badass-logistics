Printing Press Movers

A press is a line of precision units that have to go back together in exactly the right order, at exactly the right height, lined up to each other. We tear presses and bindery lines down in sequence with your press technicians, rig and move every unit, and set them back on the floor ready for reassembly and registration.

Presses move in pieces and go back together in order

A sheetfed or web press isn't one machine. It's a feeder, a string of printing units, coaters and dryers, a delivery or folder, and a web of electrical, air, ink, and water connections tying them together. Each unit is heavy, tall, and top-heavy, and the whole line only works if every unit goes back at the right height and in line with the next. Move it like a single machine and you spend weeks on reassembly problems.

We plan press moves unit by unit. Before anything is disconnected, units are tagged, the line is photographed and measured, and the foundation or floor elevations at the new site are checked against what the press needs. Your OEM or independent press technicians handle the mechanical and electrical separation. Our riggers jack, skate, and lift each unit on its designed support points and move it out along a planned path.

Between plants, units are crated or wrapped as needed and moved as project freight in reassembly order, so the first unit off the truck is the first one the crew needs. At the new floor we set each unit in position for the technicians to join, level, and register.

How a printing press move actually works

Every press is different, but the order of operations is consistent:

1. Document the line

Tag every unit and component, photograph connections, and record the line's layout and elevations. Reassembly should follow a map, not memory.

2. Separate with the technicians

Press technicians disconnect electrical, air, ink, and water systems and separate the units. Cylinders and moving parts are secured for handling.

3. Rig each unit out

Units are jacked onto skates or lifted with a gantry or forklift at their designed support points, then moved along a planned, load-checked path.

4. Move in reassembly order

Units are loaded so they arrive in the order they'll be set, with protection matched to each piece.

5. Set for reassembly

Each unit is placed on its foundation or floor position for the technicians to join, level, and bring back to register.

Get the foundation right before the press arrives

Large presses often need foundations or floor areas prepared to specific flatness and elevation. If the new floor isn't ready when the units arrive, the press sits in the shipping area and the schedule slides. We check the new site early with your technicians and contractor, and schedule deliveries to the day the floor is released — staging units if needed.

Moving a single bindery machine across the building gets the same care, just a shorter plan. See how we handle machine installation and leveling for the equipment around the press.

questions

Printing press moving FAQ

How do you move a printing press?
By documenting and tagging the line, having press technicians separate the units, rigging each unit out on its support points, moving the units in reassembly order, and setting them on the new floor for reassembly and registration.
Do you disassemble and reassemble the press?
Mechanical and electrical separation and reassembly are done by OEM or independent press technicians. We work alongside them on the rigging, moving, and setting of every unit.
Can you move a press to another state?
Yes. Units are protected or crated and moved as project freight through our licensed broker and carrier partners, in the order the crew needs them.
Do you move bindery and finishing equipment too?
Yes — folders, cutters, stitchers, binders, and mailing lines, on their own or as part of a full plant move.
How much does it cost to move a printing press?
It depends on the press size and number of units, access at both sites, distance, and technician time. Send the model and both sites and we'll quote the rigging and freight.
