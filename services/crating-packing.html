Industrial Crating & Packing

A machine that survives the rig-out can still be ruined in the truck. We drain, lock, wrap, skid, and crate industrial equipment on site — built around the machine's real weight and center of gravity, and packed for how it's actually going to travel.

Quick answer: Industrial crating and packing is the preparation of machines and equipment for transport — draining fluids, locking moving parts, protective wrapping, and building skids or custom wooden crates sized to the load. Badass Logistics crates and packs equipment on site or at staging as part of a rigging or project-freight job, including export packing with ISPM-15 compliant heat-treated lumber when freight is leaving the country.

Packing built around the machine, not the box

Most shipping damage on industrial equipment doesn't happen in a crash. It happens slowly: a machine that shifts an inch every time the truck brakes, a crate built too weak for the weight on top of it, moisture that condenses on bare ways over a two-week ocean transit, a forklift driver who picks a crate from the side where the load is heaviest. Good packing is designed to stop each of those before they start.

Because we're riggers first, our crating starts with the same numbers our rigging plans start with — the equipment's weight, its center of gravity, its lift and tie-down points, and what parts can't take load. The skid gets bolted to the machine's real mounting points. The crate is sized so fork entry lines up with the weight, and the center of gravity gets marked on the outside so the next person handling it knows where it is.

Crating is almost always part of something bigger: a machine move between plants, a plant relocation, or equipment staged after a container unload before its final delivery. We do it on site at your facility or at a staging location, timed to the rig-out and the trucks.

crate or not

Does your equipment need a crate?

Not everything needs a full crate, and over-packing costs time and money too. The right level of protection depends on the equipment and the trip:

Skid and wrap

Rugged machines moving a short distance on air-ride or enclosed equipment often only need a proper skid, protective wrap, and good securement.

Open crate

A frame crate protects edges, controls panels, and projections from impact while keeping weight down — common for domestic moves of mid-size machines.

Closed crate

Sensitive, high-value, or open-deck freight gets fully enclosed, with barrier wrap and desiccant inside when moisture is a risk.

Export packing

Freight leaving the country needs wood packaging that meets ISPM-15 (heat-treated and marked), protection for long transit times and humidity swings, and marking that survives multiple handoffs.

before the lid goes on

Machine prep that prevents damage

Packing starts with the machine itself. Before anything goes in a crate, the equipment is prepped the way its builder expects:

  • Coolant, oil, hydraulic, and water reservoirs drained or secured
  • Axes, spindles, tables, and carriages locked with shipping brackets or blocking
  • Doors, guards, and loose components secured, removed, or tagged and packed
  • Exposed machined surfaces cleaned, protected, and wrapped
  • Tilt and shock indicators installed on sensitive equipment
  • Weight and center of gravity marked on the outside of the crate

Our guides on preparing a machine for shipping and blocking, bracing, and dunnage go deeper into both.

Crating & packing FAQ

What is ISPM-15?
An international standard for wood packaging material in cross-border shipping. Lumber used for crates and skids must be treated (commonly heat-treated) and stamped with the certification mark. We use compliant lumber for export crating.
Can you crate a machine at our facility?
Yes. Most of our crating happens on site, right after the rig-out, so the machine is only handled once before it goes on the truck.
Do I need a crate for a domestic machine move?
Not always. Many domestic moves only need a skid, protective wrap, air-ride equipment, and proper securement. We recommend the lightest packing that actually protects the machine.
How do you protect machines from rust in transit?
Clean and oil exposed surfaces, then use VCI wrap or sealed barrier bags with desiccant, especially for long transits, ocean freight, or equipment going into storage.
Do you crate equipment as a standalone service?
Crating is part of our project freight work — tied to a move, relocation, install, or equipment package — rather than one-off crate orders.
