## What the numbers actually say

**1. "Crane and rigging services" is the whole ballgame.** 1,600
searches a month, *Low* competition, and a $3.00 entry bid. It is three
times the next biggest term and among the cheapest. The first draft of
this plan had crane in the smallest budget — exactly backwards.

**2. Medical cannot absorb a large budget.** "Medical equipment movers"
is 210/mo nationally and every device-specific term (MRI, CT, hospital,
imaging) is below Google's reporting floor. Narrowed to 20 metros that
is roughly 75 searches a month. It is worth running — the clicks are
high-value and nobody is bidding — but it physically cannot spend
$10/day. It needs a high bid and a small share, not a big budget.

**3. Dispatch has a much better anchor than we were using.** "Truck
dispatch company" is 480/mo at a $1.99 entry bid. "Dispatch services for
trucking companies" is 30/mo at $6.51 — sixteen times less volume at
three times the price. Anchor on the former.

**4. Bids have to go up.** Entering at $3.50–6.00 instead of $1.96 means
fewer clicks for the same money — roughly 130–145 clicks/month rather
than 250 — but they will be clicks from the top of the page instead of
impressions nobody sees. Ad Rank, not budget, was the binding
constraint.

## Competitor teardown — Freight Girlz (dispatch)

Leads with **"U.S.-Based Truck Dispatch Services"**, serves
owner-operators *and* fleets, publishes percentage pricing (6–8% of
linehaul), and stacks trust signals: 5/5 from 36 owner-operators, a live
dispatch log, BBB.

Where we differ honestly: **our rigging and project work generates the
freight**. They source from load boards and brokers. That is a real
difference and it is the thing the dispatch ads should say.
